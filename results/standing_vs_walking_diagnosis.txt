6. LIKELY TECHNICAL REASONS FOR CONFUSION IN REAL-WORLD TESTING
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. STATIC AMBIGUITY (Core Biomechanical Limitation):
   Walking is inherently a dynamic process defined by motion over time.
   In any static single video frame, a walking person frequently passes through
   a pose where both legs are straight and feet are grounded—geometrically indistinguishable
   from an upright standing pose. Without velocity or temporal displacement across frames,
   a static classifier cannot reliably tell whether a still pose is holding still or in mid-stride.

2. OVER-RELIANCE ON ABSOLUTE SCREEN COORDINATES:
   Feature importance analysis reveals that the Random Forest model derives:
   - 75.8% of its decision importance from raw absolute coordinates (x, y, z, visibility)
   - 22.0% from relative landmarks
   - Only 2.2% from distances and joint angles
   Because the user recorded standing at one specific position/depth in front of the camera
   and walking with slight step displacement or closer to the lens, the decision trees learned
   positional/scale thresholds (e.g., shoulder_width ~ 0.19 or landmark_11_y ~ 0.11) rather
   than intrinsic motion patterns. In live testing, simply standing slightly closer or in a
   different part of the webcam frame triggers branches trained on the walking recording.

3. SITTING VS. STANDING/WALKING CONTRAST:
   Sitting is recognized accurately because sitting fundamentally alters static body geometry:
   hip-to-knee vertical alignment changes drastically and absolute body height drops significantly.
   Standing and walking share almost the same upright torso and leg geometry, making them
   the primary point of failure for a static single-frame classifier.
