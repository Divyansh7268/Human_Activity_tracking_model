1. OVERALL LOSO GENERALIZATION FINDINGS
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Leave-One-Subject-Out (LOSO) cross-validation evaluated 4 distinct individuals
(21,211 frames across 16 sequences) using 158 normalized spatial and temporal features.

Overall Performance Summary:
  • Random Forest Mean Accuracy       : 66.26% ± 11.34%
  • Random Forest Mean Macro F1       : 64.84% ± 13.13%
  • Logistic Regression Mean Accuracy : 60.97% ± 11.87%
  • Logistic Regression Mean Macro F1 : 60.29% ± 12.42%

Core Empirical Takeaway:
  While within-subject evaluations previously achieved >98% accuracy, true subject-
  independent generalization drops to ~65% Macro F1. This performance gap is driven
  NOT by posture shape (sitting generalizes at 99.8% F1), but by subject-to-subject
  variability in camera framing, lower-limb tracking visibility, and dynamic thresholding.

7. INVESTIGATION OF THE STANDING PROBLEM (EVIDENCE VS HYPOTHESIS)
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
[1. OBSERVED EVIDENCE]
  - S01 Standing Foot Visibility (landmark_32) : 0.455 ± 0.251 (median 0.515)
  - S02 Standing Foot Visibility (landmark_32) : 0.978 ± 0.017 (median 0.982)
  - S03 Standing Foot Visibility (landmark_32) : 0.964 ± 0.020 (median 0.971)
  - S04 Standing Foot Visibility (landmark_32) : 0.976 ± 0.018 (median 0.982)
  - S01 Standing Ankle Visibility (landmark_28): 0.552 vs ~0.98 for S02-S04.
  - Random Forest Top Features: 6 of the top 12 features were lower-body visibility scores.

[2. PLAUSIBLE SCIENTIFIC EXPLANATION]
  - In Subject S01, the subject stood closer to the webcam, causing feet and ankles to be
    partially occluded by the bottom frame edge (visibility ~0.45-0.55).
  - When lower limbs are occluded, MediaPipe infers joint positions with high variance and noise.
  - In training on S02/S03/S04, lower limb visibility dropped primarily during active locomotion (running).
  - The decision trees leveraged low lower-body visibility as a surrogate rule for 'running'.
  - When tested on S01 standing, the model triggered this running rule, creating the 80.9% running error.

[3. UNSUPPORTED SPECULATION]
  - It was previously speculated that camera vibrations caused the S01 standing failure.
  - Examination shows head and shoulder landmarks did NOT exhibit high jitter in S01.
  - The failure was driven by camera truncation of lower limbs and visibility feature reliance,
    not mechanical vibration of the webcam hardware.

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
8. METHODOLOGICAL & PIPELINE LIMITATIONS
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  1. Visibility Scores as Model Features:
     Treating MediaPipe raw visibility as a predictive feature introduces severe camera framing
     bias, because whether a foot is visible depends on camera distance/crop, not activity.
  2. Static Frame-Rate / Window-Agnostic Speed:
     Instantaneous frame-to-frame speed (dt ~ 0.048s) is highly sensitive to single-frame keypoint
     jitter. Windowed temporal smoothing (e.g. 0.5s or 1.0s window) is needed.
  3. Small Subject Count (N=4):
     A 4-subject dataset means each training fold sees only 3 individuals' gait styles,
     making the classifier susceptible to idiosyncrasies of individual subjects.
